What is driving the change in the dollar bill?

The USD change splits exactly (in log points ≈ %) into three parts: general inflation at home, utilities moving faster or slower than that inflation, and the local currency's move against the dollar. A weakening currency pulls the dollar bill down even when local prices soar.

Summary

Findings

    Possible drivers to check against the data

    • The 2022 energy shock. Russia's invasion of Ukraine sent European gas and power prices to records. Watch whether European capitals peak in 2022–23 and ease after.
    • Price caps and subsidies. Regulated or subsidised tariffs (Gulf states, Egypt, France's tariff shield, the UK price cap) can hold bills below inflation, then jump when support is withdrawn.
    • Currency collapses. Large depreciations (e.g. Argentine peso, Egyptian pound, Japanese yen) can make dollar bills fall while local bills soar. Question 4 isolates this.
    • Climate demand. Heating-heavy (Europe, Ottawa) and cooling-heavy (Gulf, Asia) cities respond to different fuel prices and weather years.
    • Data caveat. Numbeo figures are crowd-sourced snapshots of an "85 m² apartment" bill, so small samples can swing a single year.
